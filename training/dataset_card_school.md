# {{name}}

**{{n_total}} synthetic German school materials**, one per topic, from primary school to the upper secondary level
(Abitur) across 70 school subjects. Each record contains a full text (one A4 page or less, depending on the content
type) together with a title, a description, keywords, question-answer pairs, competence-oriented learning objectives,
short search queries and **named entities with character spans** derived from that text, plus educational metadata
(subject, school level, grade band, content type, target group, didactic function, social form, KMK requirement area,
special educational needs).

The pairs can be used for tasks such as **full text → title, description, keywords, learning objectives**,
**search query → material** or **named-entity recognition** on educational texts. All texts were generated by a large
language model; none of them is copied from existing educational materials.

It is the school counterpart of
[synthetic-german-educational-materials-university-100k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JanSchachtschabel/synthetic-german-educational-materials-university-100k)
and extends
[synthetic-german-educational-materials-50k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/JanSchachtschabel/synthetic-german-educational-materials-50k),
which covers all education sectors with a smaller number of materials per sector. All three datasets share the same
column schema, so they can be combined; topics may overlap. The column `entities` is new in this dataset and empty in
the two others.

|---|---|
| `id` | record ID |
| `education_sector` | always `school` |

Columns for other education sectors (`occupation`, `competence`, `programme_area`, `early_childhood_area`,

Controlled values are given as labels together with their URIs, following the OpenEduHub / edu-sharing metadata

### Entities

Each entry in `entities` has these fields:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| `name` | full, common form in the nominative singular (e.g. „Johann Wolfgang von Goethe“), otherwise the form in the text |
| `type` | one of 36 fixed types (German labels, see table) |
| `category` | one of seven categories, derived from the type |
| `kind` | `proper_name` (a specific individual: person, place, work, law …) or `term` (a central subject or teaching term) |
| `text` | the form exactly as it appears in the full text (e.g. „Goethes“) |
| `fictional` | `true` for persons, figures and organisations invented for this material (names in examples, dialogues and tasks) |
| `spans` | every occurrence of `text` in `fulltext` as `[start, end)` character offsets (whole words only) |

Rules: all proper names are listed (first), terms only where central to the material (at most 15); every `text` was
verified to occur as a whole word in the full text, and all spans were checked against it. Persons with a name (also
invented ones such as „Lena“, „Frau Nowak“) are always `Person`; `Rolle` is used for unnamed roles („die Lehrkraft“).
Terms such as „Delta“ or „Finanzkrise“ are `Fachbegriff`, not a proper-name type. Text passages (a learning objective,
task or experimental procedure) are not entities. Real persons are mostly historical or public figures named in the
subject matter (e.g. Martin Luther, Immanuel Kant, Otto von Bismarck). Entities are not linked to GND or Wikidata.

{{ent_total}} entities in total, median {{ent_median}} per material; {{ent_proper}} are proper names and
{{ent_fictional}} are fictional. {{ent_none}} materials have no entities.

## How the data was created

1. **Topics.** School topics come from three sources, each assigned to school subjects and school levels:
   - *WirLernenOnline topic trees* of the subject portals (OpenEduHub vocabulary `oehTopics`, CC0).
   - *Keywords* of WirLernenOnline school records that match a subject heading of the Gemeinsame Normdatei (GND);
     keywords without a GND match were not used.
   - *WirLernenOnline topic abstraction:* short subject headings abstracted by the project from WirLernenOnline records,
     with the subject and school level of those records. Only topics were used, **no contents** of WirLernenOnline
     records; every abstracted topic passed a check that it does not copy the source wording.

   Topic type, school levels and subjects were assigned by three independent LLM votes per topic and cross-checked
   with the levels and subjects of the WirLernenOnline records. Nearly identical topics within a subject (embedding
   similarity ≥ 0.97) were merged; one material was generated per remaining topic.
2. **Seeds.** Each seed combines one topic with metadata: subject, school level and grade band, content type, target
   group, text type and media form, plus optional controls such as didactic function, working time, social form,
   KMK requirement area and special educational need, an opening type and invented names for people in the text.
   Content types were rotated so that each fits the topic, level and subject portal.
3. **Materials.** A language model ({{models}}, via the Anthropic Message Batches API) wrote the full text from the
   seed in the role of an author of school materials, with a language profile matching the grade band. In one
   further call it then derived title, description, keywords, question-answer pairs, learning objectives, search
   queries and entities **only from the full text** and the fixed metadata, without seeing the seed's topic, so that
   the pairs reflect the task "text → metadata". Fields that failed validation were requested again individually.
   Finally the content type was detected independently, without seeing the requested content type.
4. **Validation.** Every output was checked (length, format, number of keywords, valid taxonomy levels and entity
   types, entity text present in the full text) and requested again if unusable, up to three times per field.
   Titles and descriptions that named the material type or started with a stock phrase were requested again, but
   accepted on the last attempt; a subtitle after a colon or dash was cut off. {{n_total}} of the seeds yielded a
   complete material; the remaining ones (mostly titles that remained unusable) were dropped.{{title_note}}

Every record in the original data carries the source and method of each field; this export contains the fields
useful for training. The assignment of topics to the KMK educational standards is kept locally and not part of this
export. Export date: {{export_date}}.

## Intended use

- Training and evaluating models that generate titles, descriptions, keywords and learning objectives for German
  school resources.
- Training and evaluating retrieval models with the search queries (query → material).
- Named-entity recognition and term extraction on German educational texts (with character spans).
- Question generation and question answering on educational texts.
- Research on metadata enrichment for open educational resources.

## Limitations

- **Synthetic texts.** The materials were not reviewed by teachers or subject experts and may contain factual errors.
  They are not intended to be used as teaching materials.
- **Single model, single style.** All texts come from one language model and one set of prompts; real materials are
  more diverse in style, structure and quality.
- **Length.** Full texts are at most about one A4 page. Real worksheets, slides or web pages are often longer and contain
  navigation or layout residue; preprocess them (cleaning, shortening) before applying a model trained on this data.
- **German context.** The texts are in German and refer to the German school system.
- **Similar topics.** Most topics were abstracted from WirLernenOnline records; many school topics recur in several
  subjects or levels, so {{dup_titles}} of the materials share their title with another material (e.g. „Lineare
  Funktionen“), with different texts.
- **Uneven coverage.** The topics follow what is indexed in WirLernenOnline: history, politics and mathematics are
  covered in much more depth than smaller subjects. Some subject assignments taken from the WirLernenOnline records are
  loose (e.g. a media literacy topic filed under chemistry).
- **Entities.** Entity lists come from the same model and were checked only for presence in the text and for valid
  types, not for completeness or correct typing; terms (`kind` = `term`) are a selection.
